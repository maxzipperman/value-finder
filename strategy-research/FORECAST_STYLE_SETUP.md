## Timing and target

- Decision time is 19:30 Pacific on the day before kickoff's Eastern calendar date, matching the existing MOS replay's lead definition. Both MOS cycles must be on their stated lead dates, in chronological order, published by the decision with the existing conservative five-hour publication lag, and before kickoff.
- Style uses `team_games.parquet`'s `pass_rate_sit`. Each team contributes the mean of its last four regular-season games in the same season, with at least three available. A game's statistics become eligible 48 hours after its UTC game date. Current-game and not-yet-available rows are excluded; the audit lists contributing game IDs and latest availability times.
- The reporting delay is a fixed conservative proxy, not proof of the provider's historical publication timestamps. These derived statistics may include later provider corrections; they are not a true archived vintage. The feature audit exposes the chronological construction and this limitation remains in the interpretation.
- Target: final total points minus the consensus closing total in the existing MOS replay. The close is a **diagnostic benchmark**, not a line available when the forecast was issued, and is not an input feature. No executable ROI or CLV is claimed. The tables already select games with historical coverage and results; this is not a population-wide sample of all games.
- All parquet reads project named columns and apply `season <= 2025` before the reader returns rows. Preflight does not request total points, closing totals, wins or profit. Input fingerprints are over the filtered, projected rows, never whole files containing sealed rows. The physical parquet implementation can decode a row group internally; no 2026 row is returned to study code.

## Fixed evaluation

1. Expanding chronological folds: train on earlier seasons and evaluate the next season. At least five distinct prior seasons, 300 training games and 25 validation games are required. Missing features/targets and skipped folds are recorded.
2. Ridge regression, penalty 10, with an unpenalized intercept. Feature means and population standard deviations come only from the training fold. No tuning or use of validation labels in fitting.
3. Primary comparison is the mean across validation seasons of the per-game squared-error improvement (control minus candidate), with every validation season weighted equally. Positive means the extra feature helps. At least five validation seasons are required for inference.
4. Report a two-sided t test on season-level paired mean improvements, raw p, p multiplied by the current project count (capped at 1), and an interval adjusted for that count. Historical re-use makes these diagnostic statistics, not an independent confirmation or an authorization to bet.
5. A fixed-seed, 10,000-draw resampling interval resamples seasons and then whole Eastern game days within each selected season. Its 95% interval is diagnostic; it is not an alternate significance gate. The same paired games are kept together.

A promising result only nominates a hypothesis for a separately recorded price-based test and future untouched validation. A null result stays in the record. No automatic ranking-driven model replacement is implemented.
